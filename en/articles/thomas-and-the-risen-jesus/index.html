Thomas and the risen Jesus: what does John say about doubt?

Thomas hears from the other disciples that they have seen Jesus, but asks for an encounter of his own. In John 20:24–29, Jesus comes again and addresses what Thomas said. Thomas responds by calling him his Lord and God. The episode leads into Jesus’ words about those who believe through testimony and John’s explanation of why he wrote.

Begin with the meeting Thomas missed

The preceding scene takes place with the disciples gathered behind closed doors. Jesus stands among them, speaks peace and shows his hands and side. They rejoice. John then tells us that Thomas was absent. That gives the next conversation its setting. The other disciples tell Thomas what they experienced together. Read both scenes before deciding how to describe his response.

What Thomas asks for

Thomas names what he wants to see and touch. His words concern the wounds of the crucified Jesus, whose death the Gospel has already narrated. The request is specific. John records it as part of a conversation among disciples who have come through the same loss but now have different experiences to relate. If reading with someone else, ask what Thomas has heard and what remains difficult for him to receive.

How Jesus addresses him

Eight days later, Thomas is with the others. Jesus again comes among them and speaks peace. He invites Thomas to look at his hands and reach toward his side, responding to the terms of Thomas’s earlier words. John next gives Thomas’s confession. The narrative leaves the act of touching unreported. Attend to the response the writer actually records and to the person to whom Thomas addresses it.

Why the final words turn toward later readers

Jesus speaks of Thomas having seen and believed, then blesses those who believe without seeing. John follows the encounter with a statement about his written account. He says that the signs selected for the book are written so readers may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name. This gives readers who were absent from the room a place within the chapter’s purpose. They receive the account through words passed on to them.

A practical way to read the conversation

Read verses 19–31 and mark three movements: the first meeting, Thomas’s conversation with the disciples and the meeting where he is present. Write down what changes between them. For a group discussion, let each participant choose a sentence and explain how its place in the sequence affects its meaning. A question about the text can be stated openly and checked against the surrounding verses. At the end, reread John’s statement of purpose and consider why he places it after this encounter.
