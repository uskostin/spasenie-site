What does repentance mean to Baptists?

In the Baptist World Alliance beliefs statement, repentance of sin appears alongside personal faith in Jesus Christ when it describes believers' baptism. A separate BWA resolution connects repentance with conversion, faith, baptism, and a life of discipleship. These sources describe a response to God, not a required public display of emotion.

For the cited Baptist sources, repentance belongs to turning toward Christ in faith. It cannot be measured simply by tears, the volume of a prayer, or how much of a private story someone tells at a meeting.

Where does the word appear in the sources?

The BWA statement says believers' baptism publicly expresses discipleship for people who have repented of sin and come to personal faith in Christ. The 2004 BWA resolution describes conversion through the work of the Holy Spirit and places repentance, faith, baptism, belonging to the church, and discipleship in its account. These are statements made by a worldwide Baptist organization. A BWA resolution is not a binding doctrinal rule for every local Baptist congregation, and it is not presented here as Spasenie's own statement of faith.

The same pairing of repentance and baptism appears in Acts 2:37–41. After hearing Peter's message, people ask what they should do. Peter calls them to repent and be baptized. It is worth reading the surrounding chapter rather than treating one line as a complete script for every conversation or every person's experience.

Is repentance the same as feeling sorry?

Sorrow may accompany someone taking wrongdoing seriously, but the published statements do not offer a visible emotion as their test. Their language joins repentance to faith in Christ and to a changed course of discipleship. Someone may speak openly, while another may need time to read, listen, and ask careful questions. Neither outward style lets a stranger see the whole of a person's inner life.

This distinction is especially important when a visitor hears unfamiliar language. A person can ask, “What do you mean by sin?” or “How does turning to God relate to trusting Christ?” A useful answer should explain the words and point to the passages being read. It need not demand an immediate personal disclosure as proof that the question was sincere.

Why do Baptist sources mention baptism?

Baptists are known for believers' baptism. The BWA statement describes it as a public declaration of discipleship by people who have repented and come to personal faith. That wording helps explain why repentance is discussed when someone asks about baptism. It does not tell us how every congregation conducts preparation, interviews, or the service itself; those practical details should be checked with the particular church.

Nor does a short article settle every Christian question about the precise relation between repentance, faith, baptism, and salvation. The cited sources establish what these named documents say. If you want to understand a local church, ask its leaders how they read Acts 2 and what they ask of someone considering baptism. A church's answer deserves to be heard in its own words.

What if a person is unsure or worried?

There is no need to arrive at a first service with a rehearsed testimony. Visiting to listen is different from announcing a decision. You can ask a pastor whether a conversation can be private, who will hear your story, and what will happen next. If a question touches painful experiences, you can say that you are not ready to discuss details. Respectful explanation and a person's ability to ask questions matter in conversations about faith.

At the same time, repentance in the cited sources is not merely changing a label or joining a group. It is discussed as part of a response to God and faith in Christ. If you are exploring that meaning, reading a biblical passage with someone patient may be more helpful than searching for a single dramatic feeling. You can take notes, ask for clarification, and return to the conversation later.

How can I read about it for myself?

Start with Acts 2:37–41, then read more of Peter's speech in the same chapter. Notice the people's question, Peter's answer, and the mention of baptism. Next read the BWA beliefs statement and its short 2004 resolution. Ask what each document actually says and what it leaves unexplained. If another person gives a more detailed account, ask which part comes from Scripture and which part is that person's or congregation's interpretation.

It may help to keep three questions separate: What does the biblical passage say? What does a named Baptist source say? What does a particular church teach and practice? The answers may overlap, but one source should not silently stand in for another. That approach is especially useful when looking for a Russian-speaking church in the Orlando area and wanting to understand its teaching before deciding whether to visit again.

Common questions

Must repentance look emotional?

The cited BWA documents connect repentance with faith and discipleship; they do not prescribe tears or a public emotional display.

Does a visitor have to share a personal story?

Nothing in these sources makes telling strangers a private story a condition for visiting a service. Ask the local church how it handles conversations and privacy.

Is this Spasenie's own statement of faith?

No. This article explains published BWA documents and a biblical passage. Ask Spasenie directly about its teaching.
