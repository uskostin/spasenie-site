What does it mean to be born again? Nicodemus in John 3

In John 3, being born again means receiving new life from the Spirit. Jesus explains this to Nicodemus, a religious teacher who comes to him at night. The conversation moves from the signs Jesus performs to the source of new life, then to God’s love and the response of faith.

Why Nicodemus asks about birth

Nicodemus begins by saying what the signs have led him to conclude. He recognizes Jesus as a teacher who has come from God. Jesus directs the conversation toward seeing God’s kingdom and speaks of a birth that Nicodemus takes in a physical sense. Nicodemus asks how an adult could be born a second time. His question makes the distance between the two uses of “birth” visible to the reader.

Read the images together

In verses 5–8, Jesus speaks of water and Spirit, distinguishes birth of the flesh from birth of the Spirit, and describes the wind. You can hear it without knowing where it comes from or where it goes. That image helps the reader attend to what can be perceived even when its source is beyond ordinary control. Before deciding how to interpret the reference to water, read the whole exchange and notice how often Jesus returns to the Spirit.

Why Jesus brings up Moses

In verses 14–15, Jesus recalls Moses lifting up the serpent in the wilderness and compares that action with the lifting up of the Son of Man. The comparison leads into the promise of eternal life for those who believe. This is a turning point in the chapter. The reader has been asking what kind of birth Jesus means. The passage now directs attention to Jesus himself and to what God gives through him.

Read John 3:16 alongside the verses about light

John 3:16 speaks of God loving the world and giving his Son. The next verse describes the purpose of sending the Son in terms of saving the world. Verses 19–21 then bring in light and darkness. Notice what people do when the light comes. The passage describes people drawing near or holding back according to what the light would reveal. The opening detail about the nighttime visit is worth noticing here, while the narrator leaves Nicodemus’s reason for coming at night unstated.

A way to read the chapter with someone else

Read verses 1–8 aloud and ask each reader to name the question they hear Nicodemus asking. Then read verses 14–21 and identify what God does and how people respond. If you use two translations, compare how they render the phrase about birth in verse 3. The World English Bible reads “born anew” and notes “from above” as another rendering. Ask which sense Nicodemus hears in his reply and how the references to the Spirit help you read Jesus’ answer. End by choosing one verse to reread in its paragraph.
