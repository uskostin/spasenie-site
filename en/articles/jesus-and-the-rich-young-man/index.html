Jesus and the rich young man: what question does the meeting raise?

A wealthy man asks Jesus about eternal life. Jesus speaks about the commandments and then tells him to give to the poor and follow him. The man leaves grieving. The exchange continues with the disciples, who ask who can be saved. Reading the whole conversation helps keep their question alongside the man’s.

Where the familiar title comes from

Matthew 19 describes the man as young. Luke 18 calls him a ruler. Mark 10 describes him running up and kneeling before Jesus. The familiar title draws details from the accounts together. To read carefully, begin with one account from beginning to end. Then compare what the others add. All three connect his grief with his considerable possessions, so wealth belongs at the center of the scene.

Listen to the opening question

In Mark, the man asks what he must do to inherit eternal life. Jesus asks why he calls him good and names commandments. The man says he has kept them from his youth. Before moving on, notice that Jesus takes the question seriously and lets the man answer. Mark then says Jesus looks at him and loves him. That detail accompanies the demanding words that follow.

What Jesus asks him to do

Jesus tells him to sell what he has, give to the poor and follow him. Read the whole sentence so that giving and following stay connected. The invitation asks something concrete of this particular person. His reaction shows what makes the response difficult: he has many possessions. Keep the scene’s concrete setting when considering its implications for your own life. A decision about money calls for attention to responsibilities and to the people affected by it.

Why the disciples are astonished

After the man leaves, Jesus speaks of the difficulty wealth creates. The disciples are amazed and ask who can be saved. Jesus directs their attention to what is possible with God. Their question changes the conversation’s scale. The reader has watched one man face a costly invitation, and the disciples now ask about salvation more broadly. This is why the paragraph after the man’s departure belongs in a reading of the encounter.

Compare three passages on one page

Read Matthew 19:16–30, Mark 10:17–31 and Luke 18:18–30. Write the question, Jesus’ response, the man’s reaction and the disciples’ question in separate rows. Attach a verse reference to each observation. In a group, ask which detail made the man’s difficulty clearer to each reader. You might discuss what it means to want an answer while finding its consequences painful. Return to Mark’s mention of love when considering the tone of Jesus’ invitation.
