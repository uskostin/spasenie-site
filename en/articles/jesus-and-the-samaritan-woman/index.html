Jesus and the Samaritan woman: what happened at the well?

In John 4, Jesus meets a Samaritan woman at Jacob’s well. A request for a drink opens a conversation about the gift of God, worship and the identity of Jesus. By the end of the episode, the woman has invited her neighbors to meet him and the people who hear him call him the Savior of the world.

Why the request surprises her

John sets the scene near Sychar. Jesus is tired from the journey, and his disciples have gone into town to buy food. When he asks the woman for a drink, she names the difference between them: he is a Jew and she is a Samaritan. Her question makes the social distance part of the conversation.

What Jesus means by living water

The woman sees a deep well and notices that Jesus has nothing to draw with. She asks where he would get living water and brings up Jacob, who gave them the well. Jesus describes the water he gives as a spring leading to eternal life. Read verses 10–15 together. The woman’s answer shows that she is still thinking about thirst and making the trip to draw water, even as Jesus speaks about life in a wider sense.

How the subject turns to worship

Jesus asks the woman to call her husband. She answers, and Jesus confirms the truth of what she says while showing that he knows more about her circumstances. John leaves much of her history untold. Follow her next words: she recognizes him as a prophet and asks about a real point of disagreement, whether worship belongs on the mountain she names or in Jerusalem. Jesus acknowledges the place of the Jews in the story of salvation and speaks of worshiping the Father in spirit and truth.

From the woman’s question to the town’s encounter

The woman speaks of the coming Messiah and Jesus identifies himself to her. When the disciples return, she leaves her jar and goes into town. Her invitation points people to someone she has met and gives them a question to consider. Many initially believe because of what she tells them. After Jesus stays with them, the townspeople point to what they have heard for themselves. Read verses 28–30 and 39–42 together to see how her testimony leads into their encounter.

A way to read the chapter in one sitting

Mark the questions the woman asks and the words she uses to address or describe Jesus. Notice when she speaks of him as a Jew, when she calls him a prophet, and when she asks her neighbors whether he could be the Christ. Then read the disciples’ exchange about food in verses 31–38. Their misunderstanding echoes the exchange about water, and Jesus uses the image of a harvest while people are coming out of the town. These two conversations help connect the personal meeting at the well with the response that follows.
