Who are Baptists?

A short guide to Baptist beliefs, worship, and the questions worth asking before you visit a church.

Baptists are Christians within the Protestant tradition. Baptist churches commonly emphasize the authority of Scripture, personal faith in Jesus Christ, and baptism following a person's own profession of faith. Local congregations may differ in worship style and organization. The Baptist World Alliance beliefs statement is a useful introduction, though it does not speak for every Baptist church.

The name draws attention to baptism, but it does not describe everything that happens in a Baptist church. People gather to pray, read Scripture, hear preaching, and care for one another. Many congregations also participate in broader associations while making decisions locally.

What do Baptists believe about baptism?

The Baptist World Alliance affirms believer's baptism by immersion as a public expression of faith in Jesus Christ. This helps explain why many Baptist congregations ask a person to profess faith before baptism. Practice can vary among churches, so the best source for a particular congregation's teaching is its own statement of faith or a conversation with its leaders.

Are all Baptist churches alike?

No single worldwide institution governs every Baptist congregation. The Baptist World Alliance history describes fellowship among churches while recognizing the independence of each local church. Associations, languages, forms of worship, and views on some questions differ. The shared name alone cannot tell you exactly how one congregation is organized.

How can you learn about a local church?

Read its own account of its beliefs and ask how worship, membership, and baptism are handled there. Common questions

Are Baptists Christians?

Yes. The Baptist World Alliance's published beliefs center on Jesus Christ and the Christian Scriptures.

Why is baptism in the name?

The name reflects the tradition's emphasis on baptism following a personal profession of faith.

Do all Baptist churches have the same structure?

No. Many make decisions locally, and their relationships with associations vary.
