Who are Baptists?

Baptists are Christians in the Protestant tradition. Their name points to baptism after a person's own profession of faith.

The Baptist World Alliance beliefs statement speaks about Jesus Christ, Scripture, and believer's baptism. It is a starting point for learning about the tradition. To understand one congregation, you also need to hear from that church itself.

At a Baptist service, people read Scripture, pray, and hear a sermon. The word “Baptist,” though, will not tell you the language of a service, when it begins, or how guests are welcomed. A congregation's own visitor information is more useful for those questions.

Why does baptism matter?

The Baptist World Alliance describes believer's baptism by immersion as a public expression of faith in Jesus Christ. It follows a personal profession of faith. Visiting a service is a separate matter: you can come, listen, and ask questions without first deciding to be baptized.

What connects different congregations?

Baptist churches cooperate through associations while retaining local decision-making. The Baptist World Alliance's history describes that combination. Worship can still look different from one church to another. If a particular belief or practice matters to you, ask the congregation you plan to visit.
