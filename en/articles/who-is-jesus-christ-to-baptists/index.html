Who is Jesus Christ to Baptists?

Jesus Christ stands at the center of the Baptist sources examined here. The Baptist World Alliance calls him Lord and Savior, fully God and fully human. Its statement speaks of his death, resurrection, and the life of discipleship. American Baptist Churches USA also confesses Jesus Christ as Savior and Lord.

For these Baptist organizations, Jesus is not simply a teacher from the past. Their published statements speak of him as the living Lord of the church. This is a shared Christian conviction in the documents named below; each congregation can explain its own teaching in its own words.

What does “Lord and Savior” mean in these sources?

The two titles appear together in both documents. “Lord” expresses allegiance to Christ and his authority over the life of the church. “Savior” refers to what the documents say about his death and resurrection. The Baptist World Alliance describes Jesus as crucified and raised from the dead; the American Baptist identity statement speaks of forgiveness and reconciliation with God through him. These are theological claims made by the sources, not a promise that reading an article will produce a particular personal result.

Words like “Savior” can sound familiar while remaining unclear. A useful question is: “What does your church mean when it says Jesus saves?” Ask which passages it reads and how it connects them to the statement. This keeps the conversation close to both the text and the people who use the words.

Why say fully God and fully human?

The Baptist World Alliance uses both descriptions in the same sentence. Its statement holds together Jesus' divine identity and his real human life. The point matters because someone seeking to understand Baptist belief may otherwise hear only one side: a moral example or a distant divine figure. The source speaks of the person of Christ in both ways.

An article of this length cannot settle the long history of Christian discussion about those words. A visitor can begin by asking why the statement uses each description and where the speakers see it in Scripture. The Gospels tell of Jesus' actions, conversations, suffering, and death. A church may also direct you to other New Testament passages when explaining its confession. Let the passages and the explanation be heard together.

Why are the death and resurrection so prominent?

The Baptist World Alliance names both events when it summarizes its belief about Christ. American Baptist Churches USA likewise describes the death and resurrection of Jesus as central to the good news it proclaims. It connects those events with forgiveness and reconciliation with God. That explains why a conversation about Baptists cannot be reduced to their name or the way they practice baptism.

If you have not read a Gospel before, take one account in order rather than beginning with a list of isolated verses. Notice what leads up to Jesus' death and how the account speaks about his resurrection. You can write down the parts you do not understand. A question about what a passage actually says is a good place to start, even if you are unsure what you believe about it.

How does this connect with following Jesus?

In the Baptist World Alliance statement, Jesus' life shows a way of discipleship. The American Baptist identity statement also connects allegiance to Christ with the life and witness of believers. “Discipleship” is a church word for learning to follow Jesus. Its everyday meaning deserves explanation from the people who use it: what do they read, how do they pray, and how do they make decisions together?

Those questions are more useful than assuming every Baptist congregation arranges its meetings or ministries in the same way. Published statements describe convictions. Local churches put them into practice within their own communities. If you are visiting a church near Orlando, the congregation can tell you how it teaches the Gospel and how it welcomes questions after a service.

Where should a first-time reader begin?

Start with one of the four Gospels and read beyond a single quotation. Ask who is speaking, what has happened just before a scene, and how the people in the account respond to Jesus. Then compare what you have read with the words used in the Baptist sources. The exercise will not answer every question at once, but it gives a conversation a common text.

Bring a specific question rather than feeling obliged to know every term. “Why do you call Jesus Lord?” or “What does the resurrection mean in this passage?” is enough. If an explanation moves too quickly, ask the speaker to show the verse again. Careful questions are welcome in a serious discussion of faith.

Common questions

Do Baptists believe Jesus is God?

The Baptist World Alliance statement describes Jesus Christ as fully God and fully human.

Do these sources affirm the resurrection?

Yes. Both the Baptist World Alliance and American Baptist Churches USA speak of Jesus' resurrection in their published statements.

Is Jesus only an example of good conduct?

His life is described as a guide for discipleship, and the same source calls him Lord and Savior. Those claims belong together in the published statement.
