Martha and Mary: what does Jesus say about worry?

In Luke 10:38–42, Martha welcomes Jesus into her home. Mary sits at his feet and listens. Martha asks Jesus to tell her sister to help with the serving, and he answers by naming the worries that have taken hold of her attention. He also affirms Mary’s choice to listen.

Begin with Martha’s welcome

Luke introduces Martha through what she does for Jesus: she receives him into her house. That detail matters when you read his answer later. Hospitality is what brings the conversation into this home in the first place. Notice when the description shifts. Luke mentions the work of serving as it begins to pull Martha’s attention in several directions. Then she speaks directly to her guest. You can trace those changes without giving either sister a personality label.

What Martha asks Jesus to do

Martha calls attention to the fact that she is serving alone and asks Jesus to send Mary to help. The request is practical, and the question that introduces it also tells us that Martha wonders whether Jesus cares. Read her words aloud once with the emphasis on the work and once with the emphasis on that question. Both concerns are there. Jesus responds to Martha herself and says she is worried and troubled about many things. Luke gives no further account of how the household work was divided.

What Mary chooses

Mary’s posture at Jesus’ feet is paired with a simple action. She listens to what he says. Jesus calls her choice good and says it will remain hers. The scene gives readers time to ask what it means to receive someone whose words they want to hear. A host may be thinking about what a guest needs, and a listener may be attending to what the same guest has come to say. The short account lets both activities appear in one room. Jesus’ answer gives the act of listening particular weight in that moment.

Read it after the Good Samaritan

The preceding scene in Luke 10 ends with Jesus telling a questioner to act like the person who showed mercy. Then the story moves into Martha’s house. If you read the scenes in sequence, you have a concrete act of care fresh in mind when you hear Jesus affirm Mary’s attention to his words. Ask what each scene brings into focus. The Samaritan sees someone in need and responds. In the house, Mary hears Jesus while Martha finds herself pulled by much serving. Each scene invites attention to the person in front of you.

Use the scene in a group discussion

Read all five verses together. Ask one reader to retell the scene starting with Martha’s welcome, another to follow Mary’s actions, and a third to read Jesus’ answer. Mark what Luke actually says before discussing motives. Then consider an ordinary visit where preparations begin to take up all the time you meant to spend with someone. What could you simplify or share so you can sit down and listen? Treat the example as an invitation to look at your own situation. The Gospel gives its own details, and the group can return to those details when different readings arise.
